Defining Traffic Congestion
on OR-99W Southbound

Interactive exploration of speed patterns, spatial-temporal congestion, and threshold sensitivity along OR-99W SB through Tigard, King City, Tualatin, and Sherwood.

About This Project

The data comes from INRIX probe vehicle sensors along OR-99W Southbound, covering 33 road segments across Washington County, Oregon. Speed observations are collected at 5-minute resolution and aggregated to 15-minute intervals for analysis.

The question is deceptively simple: what does "congested" mean? There is no single agreed definition. This project compares threshold-based definitions (speed below X mph, or below X% of free-flow) against data-driven cluster-based definitions on the same real corridor data.

Key findings so far include strong daily periodicity (clear rush-hour slowdowns), spatial heterogeneity across the four cities (Sherwood runs fastest, Tigard is most congested), and distinct weekday vs. weekend patterns. Cluster analysis is ongoing.

Methods
Data processing & analysis notes
  • Source: INRIX probe vehicle speed data, OR-99W SB, Washington County, OR. April 19–26, 2026 (Hovmöller); January 2025 (clustering analysis).
  • Aggregation: Raw 5-minute observations are averaged to 15-minute intervals per segment using the floor of the timestamp minute (0, 15, 30, 45).
  • Segments: 33 sequentially ordered segments cover Tigard (1–15), King City (16–24), Tualatin (25), and Sherwood (26–33).
  • Hovmöller diagrams: Space-time raster with segment order on the Y-axis (reversed, northernmost at top) and time on the X-axis. Color encodes speed using a Turbo palette capped at 70 mph.
  • Free-flow reference: The reference_speed field from INRIX, representing typical uncongested speed for each segment.
  • Clustering: k-means on speed and speed/reference_speed features. Number of clusters and final solution are under evaluation.
Data: Oregon DOT / INRIX probe vehicle speeds  ·  Course: STAT 409 Data Science Practicum, Spring 2026, Portland State University
